# PROOF-CHAIN: no set of points of PG(3,9) meets every line in exactly 2 or 5 points

Status of this document: written 2026-11-10/11 for a hostile referee.  Updated 2026-11-11 after the referee audit (cover_asserts, planar_validity, ENCODER-SEMANTICS, children re-run) and 2026-11-13 after the full local regeneration of every refutation (section 5).  Not peer reviewed.
Every step carries one of four tags:

* **[H]** hand-proved: the proof is in this text (short, elementary), and, where a finite check exists, it is also repeated by a script.
* **[C]** certified: a certificate file (CNF + LRAT proof) checked by the CakeML-verified checker `cake_lpr`; the CNF is rebuilt from scratch by the stdlib script `replay/rebuild_all.py` and its SHA-256 is compared with `certs/manifest.jsonl`.
* **[S]** exact stdlib computation: a finite computation (integer arithmetic, enumeration, hashing) done by an independent stdlib script that a referee can re-run in seconds or minutes; not formally certified.
* **[L]** cited (external theorem or tool).

**Theorem (computer-assisted).**  There is no set K of points of PG(3,9) such that every line meets K in exactly 2 or exactly 5 points.
(Equivalently, by complementation, no set meeting every line in 5 or 8 points.  This is the first open case of the two-intersection problem of Tallini Scafati; source of the question: F. Ihringer, arXiv:2411.16288, Section 5.2.)

The proof is a *cover*: Section 1 reduces every hypothetical K to one of 174 level-1 cases (plus level-2 sub-cases for 9 of them; two of the nine are redundant) by hand-proved normalisations and one certified classification;
Section 2 refutes every case by a SAT proof checked by `cake_lpr`.  Nothing about prime-order symmetry is used (earlier class-by-class exclusions of prime-order symmetry are supplementary and not part of this chain).

## 0. Conventions (the exact objects that the scripts use)

* GF(9) = GF(3)[i]/(i^2+1); the element a+bi has code a+3b (0..8); 0 = code 0, 1 = code 1, -1 = code 2.  `replay/replay.py`: `e_add`, `e_mul`, `e_frob` (x -> x^3 : a+bi -> a-bi).
* PG(3,9): the 820 points are the vectors of GF(9)^4 whose first non-zero coordinate is 1, in lexicographic order of codes (index p = 0..819).  The 7,462 lines are the 2-dimensional subspaces
  (each line = the sorted tuple of its 10 point indices), **numbered in the order of their two smallest points** (this is the order in which pair covering finds them, `code/pg.py`; the replay enumerates
  lines by row-reduced matrices and sorts them: `replay/rebuild_all.py: Geometry`).  PG(2,9) (the "quotient plane" Q) is built the same way from 3-vectors (91 points, 91 lines).
* P0 = (0,0,0,1) (index of the 4-vector).  For a normalised 3-vector v (a point of Q) and t in GF(9), (v:t) = normalise(v1,v2,v3,t) is the point of height t on the line P0 v (a line through P0 has the 10 points P0 and (v:t), t in GF(9)).
  "height 0" points are (v:0).  The line of PG(3,9) through P0 in direction v is `line_of[v]`.
* K = set of the points of a hypothetical set; x_p = [p in K]; k = |K|.  A **section** of a plane pi through P0 is K intersected with pi (91 points).
  Planes through P0 <-> lines ell of Q (a plane through P0 is the union of the lines through P0 whose directions lie on ell); `line_pts[L]` = the 91 points of the plane over the quotient line number L (P0 plus the points projecting to ell).

## 1. From the problem to the star cover (level 0)

### 1.1 Parameters  [H]
Let K meet every line in alpha = 2 or beta = 5 points, |K| = k, q = 9.  With x_l = |l n K|: sum_l x_l = k(q^2+q+1) = 91k, sum_l x_l(x_l-1) = k(k-1) (every ordered pair of distinct points is on exactly one line)
and sum_l (x_l-2)(x_l-5) = 0, the number of lines being 82*91 = 7462.  Since sum_l x_l^2 = k(k-1) + 91k, the last equation reads k(k-1) + 91k - 7*91k + 10*7462 = 0, i.e.
k^2 - 547 k + 74620 = 0 (the general identity is k^2 - ((alpha+beta-1)(q^2+q+1)+1) k + alpha beta (q^2+1)(q^2+q+1) = 0), whose discriminant is 27^2:  **k = 260 or k = 287**.  (code/params.py, results/params_table.txt, also gives the line counts 4550/2912 for k = 260 and 3731/3731 for k = 287 and the plane statistics.)
Nothing else is used from the table.  *Status: [H]; exact arithmetic of 1.1-1.3 in `replay/level0_arith.py` (stdlib, `logs/level0_arith.log`); also reproduced by code/params.py.*

### 1.2 Planes and the star at a point of K  [H]
K is non-empty, so fix P0 in K (the position of P0 is made standard in 1.4).  Let pi be a plane through P0 with m = |K n pi|.  Every line of pi meets K in 2 or 5 points, so K n pi is a (2,5)-set of PG(2,9);
the same counting in the plane (91 lines, 10 points per line) gives m^2 - 61 m + 910 = 0, **m = 26 or 35** [H].
Through P0 inside pi there are 10 lines; with a two-secants and b five-secants, a + b = 10 and a + 4b = m - 1, so b = (m-11)/3 = 5 (m = 26) or 8 (m = 35): the number of two-secants through P0 in pi is **5 if m = 26 and 2 if m = 35** [H].
Let Sigma = the set of directions v (points of Q) such that the line through P0 in direction v is a two-secant.  Then |Sigma n ell| = 5 or 2 for every line ell of Q: **Sigma is a (2,5)-set of PG(2,9)**, and
the plane over ell has |K n pi| = 26 exactly when |Sigma n ell| = 5 [H].  Counting over all 91 lines through P0 (a + b = 91, a + 4b = k - 1): b = (k-92)/3, i.e.
**|Sigma| = 26 when k = 287 and |Sigma| = 35 when k = 260** [H].  (Case names: A = (k 287, |Sigma| 26), C = (k 260, |Sigma| 35).)
*Machine check of the structural facts for every class representative: `replay/star_setup.py` (Sigma is a (2,5)-set of the right size, 26 resp. 56 planes of size 26 through P0, triple non-collinear).*

### 1.3 Planar (2,5)-sets of PG(2,9) up to PGammaL(3,9): 3 classes of size 26, 7 classes of size 35  [C] + [H] + [S]
*Claim.* Every (2,5)-set of PG(2,9) of size 26 (35) is PGammaL(3,9)-equivalent to one of the 3 (7) representatives of `results/planar_classes.json` (|Aut| = 80, 26, 16 resp. 64, 40, 32, 32, 16, 16, 2).
*Proof structure* (`code/planar.py`, `planar_classify.py`):
1. [H] A (2,5)-set of size m has 5-secants (their number is 26 for m = 26 and 56 for m = 35, by the two planar moment equations).  Move one 5-secant to the line z = 0 (PGL(3,9) is transitive on lines); its section is a 5-subset R of the 10 points of that line;
   PGammaL(2,9) has exactly two orbits on 5-subsets (sizes 180 and 72, `rebuild_all.py` recomputes them), so R is one of two representatives R#0, R#1.  The translations of the affine plane z != 0 fix the line pointwise and are regular on the 81 affine points, so the origin (0,0,1) may be taken in K
   (K has 21 or 30 affine points).  What is left of the stabiliser is H_R = {diag(A,1) frob^e preserving R} (orders 64 and 160); symmetry is broken by the lex-leader constraints x <=_lex x o h, h in H_R (sound: the lex-least member of every H_R-orbit satisfies all of them).
2. [C] **Enumeration completeness.**  For (m, R#) in {26,35} x {0,1} the CNF "planar instance + units (R, origin) + lex-leader + one blocking clause per solution of `results/planar_k<m>_R<i>.json`" is UNSAT: `certs/planar_complete_k{26,35}_R{0,1}.{cnf,lrat}`
   (LRAT 37 MB, 37 MB, 2.7 GB, 1.6 GB, `s VERIFIED UNSAT`, manifest tags `planar_complete_k..`); rebuilt byte for byte by `replay/rebuild_all.py --only planar` (4 of 4 equal).  Hence the JSON lists contain every (2,5)-set in the frame, up to the symmetry of step 1.  *Validity of the lists* [S] (`replay/planar_validity.py`, log `logs/planar_validity.log`, all 1,345 solutions, nothing sampled): every listed solution is a set of the right size meeting all 91 lines of PG(2,9) in 2 or 5 points (direct check, not via the CNF), has K n l0 = R and the origin, and is the lexicographically least member of its H_R orbit against every element of H_R, so no two solutions share an orbit; H_R (orders 64 and 160) is rebuilt independently from its definition, equals the group used in `rebuild_all.planar_cnf` as a set of permutations, and its order equals the orbit-stabiliser value 8 x 1440 / (180 resp. 72); PGammaL(2,9) is generated (1,440 permutations of l0) and has exactly the two orbits 180 + 72 on the 252 five-subsets.
3. [H] + [S] (no nauty).  *Counting identity* (`replay/planar_count.py`, log `logs/planar_count.log`): the solutions of step 2 are one per H_R-orbit of the sets K with K n l0 = R and origin in K, so
   |Omega0(R#i)| = sum over solutions of |H_R|/|Stab_{H_R}(S)|; the 81 translations of AG(2,9) are regular on the affine points, so the number of (2,5)-sets with K n l0 = R is N(R) = 81 |Omega0|/(m-5); PGammaL(3,9) is transitive on lines and on each of the two orbits
   of 5-subsets of l0 (180 + 72 = 252), so the number of pairs (K, 5-secant) is 91 (180 N(R#0) + 72 N(R#1)) = N_total(m) x5(m) with x5(m) = (10m-182)/3.  Result: **N_total(26) = 9,634,464 and N_total(35) = 61,827,948** (N(R#1) = 9,882 for m = 26, N(R#0), N(R#1) = 136,728, 186,624 for m = 35).
   The class list of results/planar_classes.json predicts sum |PGammaL(3,9)|/|Aut_i| with |PGammaL(3,9)| = 84,913,920 and |Aut| = (80, 26, 16), resp. (64, 40, 32, 32, 16, 16, 2): **the same two numbers**.
   *Aut orders and inequivalence* (`replay/planar_aut.py`, log `logs/planar_aut.log`): for the fixed standard frame F0 the map g = M o frob^e -> (g(F0), e) is a bijection from the elements of PGammaL(3,9) onto the pairs (ordered projective frame F', e in {0,1}) (M is determined by F' alone because PGL(3,9) is sharply transitive on ordered frames, and frob fixes F0; this is a bijection of group elements with pairs, not an action of the group on frames alone), so, writing g_{F',e} for the element with g_{F',e}(F0) = F' and Frobenius part e, |Aut(S)| = number of pairs (F', e) with F' an ordered frame inside S and g_{F',e}(S) = S; the script computes it for the ten
   representatives and obtains exactly nauty's orders (80, 26, 16; 64, 40, 32, 32, 16, 16, 2); for the two pairs with equal order (C2, C3) and (C4, C5) it searches all frame images for an isomorphism and finds none.  *Conclusion:* the ten representatives are (2,5)-sets of the right sizes (replay/star_setup.py),
   pairwise inequivalent (distinct |Aut| or no isomorphism), their orbits have sizes |G|/|Aut_i| summing to the total number of (2,5)-sets of that size, hence they exhaust all of them: **the classification is complete, with no use of nauty** (nauty is only the source of the representatives).
   Further consistency checks: the 2-secant frame gives the same 3 classes for m = 26 (results/planar2sec_*), and the star-frame enumerations return the predicted 140 / 122 sections (m = 26) and 6,524 (m = 35) (code/star2d_check.py, star2d_check35.py).

### 1.4 Normalisation: Sigma is a representative and three second points sit at height 0  [H]
Let K be a (2,5)-set, P0 in K, Sigma = Sigma(K,P0) as in 1.2.  The stabiliser of P0 in PGammaL(4,9) contains diag(A,1) (A in GL(3,9)) and the Frobenius, acting on the directions (the points of Q) as PGammaL(3,9); so some collineation fixing P0 maps K to a (2,5)-set K'
containing P0 with Sigma(K',P0) = the representative of the class of Sigma (collineations preserve the property and the numbers of secants).  Replace K by K'.
Let v1, v2, v3 be the first non-collinear triple of the representative Sigma (in the order of point indices; Sigma has more than 10 points, so it is not contained in a line).  The line of direction v_i is a two-secant, so it contains exactly one point (v_i : t_i) of K other than P0.
The elations with centre P0, (x,x4) -> (x, x4 + r.x) (r in GF(9)^3, 729 of them), fix P0 and every line through P0, hence Sigma, and send (v_i:t) to (v_i : t + r.v_i); since v1, v2, v3 are linearly independent the map r -> (r.v1, r.v2, r.v3) is a bijection of GF(9)^3, so r can be chosen with r.v_i = -t_i.
After this replacement: **P0 in K, (v_i:0) in K for i = 1,2,3, the line through P0 in direction v is a two-secant iff v is in Sigma**, and |K| = k = 287 (A) or 260 (C).  These are the *units* U of the case (3 + 1 point units, 91 line-type units).
Conclusion: every K gives rise to a K' satisfying the units of exactly one of the ten cases A0,A1,A2 (k = 287) or C0,...,C6 (k = 260).  *Machine checks:* the units against the main code, `replay/star_setup.py --compare`; the lemma itself on the actual geometry, `replay/lemma_checks.py` (all 729 elations fix P0 and every line through P0 and act as (v:t) -> (v:t+r.v);
r -> (r.v1, r.v2, r.v3) is a bijection for the triple of every case; 300 random diag(A,1) o frob^e fix P0 and induce A o frob^e on the directions; log `logs/lemma_checks.log`).

### 1.5 The base CNF of a case  [H] (semantics) + [C] (bytes)
F(case) has the variables x_p (p = 1..820) and, for every line l, an exact DP/BDD encoding of "sum of the 10 variables of l is in {2,5}" with y_l = [sum = 5] (`cnfenc.sum_in_set`, forward and backward channeling clauses and pairwise at-most-one per layer, so every auxiliary variable is a function of x),
the DP encoding of "sum of all x_p = k", and the unit clauses U.  *Encoder correctness* [H]: by induction over the layers, the variable of layer i and partial sum s is true iff the first i inputs sum to s (forward clauses give the implication one way, backward clauses the other; the pruning keeps exactly the
sums that can still reach the allowed set); the full proof (completeness, soundness and functionality of the gadget, the local-count, unit, lex-leader and blocking clauses, with the clause-generating code quoted) is `ENCODER-SEMANTICS.md`; exhaustive tests are `replay/encoder_exhaustive.py` (all inputs of the production line/local-count shapes with independent model counting, 300 random shapes, n = 14, 16, the plane pipeline on PG(2,3) and PG(2,4), the lex gadget; sampled at the real plane/planar and 91- and 820-variable sum sizes: see its header) and the older `cnfenc.selftest()` / `rebuild_all.py --selftest`.  *Therefore:* x satisfies F(case) (with some values of the auxiliary variables) iff x is the indicator of a set K of k points with all line intersections in {2,5}, satisfying U.
File: `cnf/full_<A|C>_c<i>.cnf` (490,402 variables, about 2.57 M clauses, 50 MB), first line `c trivial group case <case> class <i> (aut <|Aut|>) k <k>`.  **[C] The stdlib script rebuilds all ten from scratch with its own GF(9), PG(3,9), star set-up and encoder and obtains the hash recorded in `results/modal_base_sha256.json`** (and the cubes below, built from it, equal the manifest hashes).

## 2. Level 1: the cover by sections of one plane (10 cases, 174 cubes)

### 2.1 The plane L1 and its sections  [H] + [C]
In each case let L1 be the first of the three quotient lines v1v2, v1v3, v2v3 over which the plane has size 26 (`|Sigma n ell| = 5`, 1.2; it exists in all ten cases, `cube_run.cubes_for` stops otherwise).  The plane pi1 contains P0 and two of the three height-0 points.
For a (2,5)-set K satisfying U, the section S = K n pi1 satisfies the **plane CNF** G(L1): 91 variables, every line of pi1 meets S in 2 or 5 points (y-encoding), |S| = m = 26, the line types of the 10 lines of pi1 through P0 (units), the point units inside pi1, and the **local counts**
[H]: a point p of pi1 inside S lies on a two-secants and b five-secants of pi1 with a + b = 10 and a + 4b = m - 1, so b = (m-11)/3; a point outside S has a + b = 10 and 2a + 5b = m, so b = (m-20)/3.  With d = (m-20)/3
the number of five-secants of pi1 through a point is d + 3 if the point is in S and d if not (m = 26: 5 resp. 2; the same formula is used for the planes of size 35 in level 2, d = 5).  So **every section satisfies G(L1)** [H] (*machine check of the local counts on the 1,345 real planar (2,5)-sets of the frame enumerations: `replay/lemma_checks.py`*).
**[C] Completeness (level 1).**  The CNF "G(L1) + one blocking clause for each of the sections in the union of the 8 homothety orbits of the cube representatives" is UNSAT, `certs/cover1_<case><i>.{cnf,lrat}` (10 certificates, manifest tags `cover1_*`, text LRAT 48-248 MB, `s VERIFIED UNSAT`, see section 5);
the blocked sets are the images of the representatives `certs/cubes_<case><i>.json` under the 8 homotheties h_lambda = diag(1,1,1,lambda), lambda in GF(9)^*, expanded by the script itself.  `replay/cover_asserts.py` (log `logs/cover_asserts.log`) asserts, for each of the ten cases: the plane L1 and the triple recomputed from Sigma; every representative lists exactly the 91 plane points (3 are fixed by base units, 88 are free: the 91-point assignment of the referee's wording is the 91 listed points, 88 free), agrees with the base units and passes a direct (2,5) section check; the 8 images are recomputed, are valid sections, fix the units, and the orbit sizes recorded (8, or 4 / 2 for one cube per case) are the recomputed ones; the stored `cover1_*.cnf` equals the rebuilt one, and its blocking clauses, parsed from the file, block exactly the union of the orbits, cube by cube; the cube CNF hash of every tag is rebuilt from the same section, which is the lambda = 1 image of its orbit.  (So the representatives are *not* trusted: if the union of their orbits missed one section the CNF would be satisfiable.)
Numbers of sections / cubes: A0 122 / 16, A1 140 / 18, A2 140 / 18, C0 122 / 16, C1 140 / 18, C2 140 / 18, C3 122 / 16, C4 140 / 18, C5 140 / 18, C6 140 / 18; total 52 + 122 = **174 cubes**.

### 2.2 The homothety reduction  [H]
h_lambda fixes P0, fixes every line through P0 (it multiplies heights by lambda, (v:t) -> (v:lambda t)), fixes the height-0 points and therefore U; it maps (2,5)-sets to (2,5)-sets.  So if K satisfies U, so does h_lambda(K), and its section on pi1 is h_lambda(S).
*Machine check (`replay/lemma_checks.py`): for all 8 lambda, h_lambda(v:t) = (v:lambda t), P0 and all height-0 points are fixed, every line through P0 is mapped onto itself, the units of the ten cases are fixed pointwise and the first plane of every case is preserved.*
The 8 homotheties preserve the list of sections of pi1; the 174 cubes are one representative per orbit.  **Conclusion of 2.1-2.2:** for a K satisfying U of case X, after replacing K by some h_lambda(K), the section K n pi1 is one of the cube representatives of X.

### 2.3 The cubes  [C]

### 3.1 Assembling the proof
Suppose K exists.  By 1.1 k is 260 or 287 and K is non-empty; fix P0 in K.  By 1.2 Sigma(K,P0) is a (2,5)-set of Q of size 26 (k = 287) or 35 (k = 260); by 1.3 it is PGammaL(3,9)-equivalent to a representative, i.e. to the one of exactly one of the cases A0-A2, C0-C6;
by 1.4 (lifting and elations) we may replace K by a (2,5)-set K' that satisfies the units U of that case, so K' satisfies the base CNF F(X) (1.5).  By 2.1 the section K' n pi1 satisfies G(L1), so by the level-1 completeness certificate it is a homothety image h(S) of a representative S, and by 2.2
h^-1(K') satisfies U and has section S: it satisfies the cube CNF of the cube (X, S).  If this cube was refuted monolithically, that is a contradiction.  Otherwise (one of the seven cubes closed through level 2, plus C2_c00 and C1_c04 which have both proofs) the section of the same set on pi2 satisfies G(L2) with the section S fixed,
so by the level-2 completeness certificate it equals a child, and the set satisfies the child CNF, which is refuted.  Every branch ends in a verified UNSAT: **K does not exist**.  The hypotheses used are exactly steps 1-13 of the table in Section 6.

## 4. Refutation certificates and their provenance  [C]
Every refutation is a pair (CNF, LRAT) with `s VERIFIED UNSAT` from `cake_lpr` ([L]: Tan, Heule, Myreen, CakeML-verified LRAT checker).  The run: `cadical -t 7200 --lrat=true --binary=true --congruence=false` (CaDiCaL 3.0.1; its answer is not trusted, only the checker's),
then `cake_lpr` in the same container; the records (`certs/manifest.jsonl`) carry sha256(CNF), sha256(LRAT), LRAT size, conflicts, CPU seconds and the verdict. ## 7. What a referee should attack first
0. Preliminary remark: the exact numbers quoted in this document come from the logs named next to them (logs/, results/modal_ledger.jsonl, certs/manifest.jsonl).
